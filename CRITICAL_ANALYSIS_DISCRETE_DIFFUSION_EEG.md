## 🚩 CRITICAL PROBLEMS DISCOVERED

### Problem #1: EEG Has Terrible Signal-to-Noise Ratio

**The Reality**:
- EEG signals are "on the order of millionth of a volt"
- "One of the primary challenges of EEG technology is that electrical activity generated by the brain is miniscule"
- "Easily contaminated by noise from diverse sources"
- Low SNR is a fundamental challenge in EEG analysis

**Why This Matters for VQ**:
From research on VQ for EEG:
> "Minor time-delayed variants are very likely to be treated as distinct tokens by the quantizer"
> "Small shift in time may produce different variants of a fixed pattern"
> "EEG signals are often noisy and corrupted due to variability during data acquisition"

**The Problem**:
- VQ is sensitive to noise
- EEG is inherently noisy
- **Bad combination** - might get unstable/inconsistent tokenization

**Impact**: VQ-VAE reconstruction quality might be poor, making discrete diffusion ineffective

---

### Problem #2: Temporal Information Loss in Discrete Tokens

**Recent Research Findings** (Feb 2025!):

From "Single-Channel EEG Tokenization Through Time-Frequency Modeling":
> "Effective tokens must represent underlying motifs that reflect distinct neural or physiological events, though these motifs are often distorted by noise, amplitude scaling, and temporal warping"

From general time series tokenization research:
> "Discretizing dynamic time series is more difficult than compressing static data, as it requires preserving temporal dependencies while reducing granularity"
> "VQ-VAE-based approaches... are optimized for compression, resulting in symbolic representations that lack awareness of temporal dynamics"

**Why This Matters**:
- EEG temporal dynamics are critical (P300 at 300ms vs 320ms matters!)
- Long-range temporal correlations (LRTC) are essential for realistic EEG
- Vector quantization "does not take into account the temporal evolution of the signals"

**The Problem**:
We might preserve spatial patterns but **lose temporal fidelity** needed for realistic EEG generation.

**Impact**: Worse quality than continuous diffusion, defeating the purpose

### Problem #4: Continuous Diffusion Might Be Fundamentally Better for Audio/EEG

**Research on Continuous vs Discrete for Audio**:
